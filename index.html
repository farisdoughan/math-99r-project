How it works

The image is resized, then converted to grayscale using 0.2126R + 0.7152G + 0.0722B, rounded to integers from 0 to 255. Transparent pixels are placed over white. Coordinates start at zero: x goes right and y goes down.

The kernel visits every pixel, left to right, one row at a time. At the borders, the nearest pixel is repeated. Kernels are applied without flipping (cross-correlation). Sobel uses raw weighted sums; the central difference includes a factor of ½ with unit pixel spacing.

Magnitude is displayed relative to the largest value in the image. Signed responses are blue for negative, white for zero, and orange for positive. Numerical calculations show the original values. No extra smoothing, thresholding, or thinning is applied.
